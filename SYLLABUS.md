# Electrochemistry — course syllabus plan

Agreed approach: use the two books as the authority on *what to cover and how
deep to go*, then write every explanation fresh. No prose, no figures and no
end-of-chapter problems are taken from either book.

## Sources

| | Book | Role in this course |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Bagotsky, *Fundamentals of Electrochemistry*, 2nd ed. (Wiley, 2006) | Solution chemistry, thermodynamics, conduction, interfaces, applied electrochemistry |
| **B** | Bard & Faulkner, *Electrochemical Methods: Fundamentals and Applications*, 2nd ed. (Wiley) | Measurement techniques, kinetics, instrumentation, surfaces |

They are complementary by design. B's preface states that classical material —
thermodynamics of cells, conductance, potentiometry — is deliberately excluded;
that material comes from A. A carries one techniques chapter, while B is built
around seven. So the split below is not arbitrary: it is where each book is the
stronger source.

## Detailed plan

### L0 — A short introduction (done)
What electrochemistry studies · charge, current and potential · how a cell is
written · the road ahead.
*Source: written fresh; the two books set the syllabus but no book
prose/figures/exercises are copied. Replaces the old "Fundamentals" primer as
agreed; no prior university chemistry assumed.*

### L1 — Ionic conduction · A ch.1, B ch.1 (rebuilt, done)
Conductors, electronic vs ionic. Ions in electrolyte solution. Conductivity and
what a cell constant really is. Circuits involving ionic conductors; what an
electrode physically is. Passage of current and electrode reactions;
classification of electrodes and reactions. Faraday's laws as charge
conservation. Mass-balance equations. Sign conventions for currents and fluxes.

### L2 — Electrode potentials · A ch.2–3, B ch.2 (done)
Interfacial potential differences (Galvani potentials) and why a *single*
electrode potential is not measurable. Exchange currents. Open-circuit voltage.
Electrode potential as a defined quantity. Cell voltage at nonzero current.
Thermodynamic functions in electrochemistry. Activity. EMF of galvanic cells.
Concentration dependence of electrode potentials. Why electrode thermodynamics
has special features.

### L3 — Solution chemistry · A ch.5,7,8 (done)
Electrolytic dissociation. Ionic solvation and hydration. Activity of real
solutions. Ion–ion interaction. Interfaces between miscible electrolytes and
diffusion potentials. Membranes and cells with transference. Nonaqueous
electrolytes, melts and solid electrolytes.

### L4 — Mass transfer and limiting currents · A ch.4, B ch.4 (done)
Fick's laws. Migration and diffusion; where each carries current. Convective
transport. Limiting diffusion current. Nernst–Planck flux equation.
Supporting electrolyte and what it suppresses. Convective mass transfer,
the hydrodynamic boundary layer, and Levich.

### L5 — Electrode kinetics and polarization · A ch.6,13,14, B ch.3 (done)
Rate-determining step. Butler–Volmer equation. Tafel slope and transfer
coefficient. Exchange current density. Activation polarization, concentration
polarization, and their superposition. Multistep reactions. Kinetic effects of
the double layer and of adsorption. Reacting producing a new phase.

### L6 — The electrical double layer · A ch.10, B ch.13 (done)
Electrical structure of the interphase. Compact and diffuse layers. Debye
length. Surface excess and adsorption isotherms. Thermodynamics of surfaces.
The mercury electrode as the model system; platinum and other real surfaces.
Capacitive vs Faradaic current.

### L7 — Measuring a potential · A ch.9,12, B (practice) (done)
Reference electrodes and how each is fixed. Potentiometry: voltage and
potential measurement, and the cell you must assemble to get it right.
Work functions and Volta potentials, and why they relate to absolute
potential. Open-circuit measurement, and what a reference cannot do.

### L8 — Steady-state and transient methods · A ch.11–12, B ch.5,8 (done)
Transient conditions and transient diffusion to large and finite electrodes.
Steady-state polarization. Basic potential step methods: chronoamperometry,
chronopotentiometry. Controlled-current techniques and coulometry.

### L9 — Sweep methods and polarography · B ch.6–7 (done)
Linear sweep and cyclic voltammetry: reading a voltammogram, peak separation,
the diagnostic for a reversible system. Polarography and the dropping mercury
electrode. Pulse voltammetry and its sensitivity advantage.

### L10 — Hydrodynamic methods · B ch.9, A ch.4.4 (done)
Rotating disk electrode: uniform, controlled, reproducible mass transport.
Rotating ring–disk. Channel and tube flow. Koutecký analysis, and
experimentally separating kinetic from transport limits.

### L11 — Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy · B ch.10 (done)
Small-signal AC response. Equivalent circuits. Faradaic and non-Faradaic
impedance. Charge-transfer resistance, double-layer capacitance, Warburg
diffusion. Nyquist and Bode plots. Interpreting an EIS spectrum of a real
cell.

### L12 — Coupled chemistry and coulometry · A ch.17–18, B ch.11–12 (done)
EC, CE and ECE mechanisms and how they distort a voltammogram. Catalytic and
mediated schemes. Bulk electrolysis. Controlled-potential coulometry.
Coulometric titration. Industrial processes and electrochemical reactor design:
separators, porous and three-dimensional electrodes.

### L13 — Corrosion · A ch.22 (done)
Why a metal reverts thermodynamically. The four requirements of a corrosion
cell. Anodic and cathodic reactions, including oxygen reduction. Passivation.
Types of corrosion and differential aeration. The galvanic series. Protection.

### L14 — Batteries, fuel cells and devices · A ch.19–21 (done)
What a battery actually stores. Performance: capacity, rate, life. Primary,
storage and lithium batteries. Electrochemical capacitors and supercapacitors.
Fuel cell design principles, PEM and direct methanol. Transducers and sensors.

### L15 — Surfaces and modified electrodes · A ch.16,27, B ch.14,16–17 (done)
Anodic and cathodic metal deposition. Surface-layer formation. Electroactive
layers and modified electrodes. Scanning probe techniques. Spectroelectrochemistry
and coupled characterisation.

### L16 — Advanced topics · A ch.24–36, B ch.18 (done)
Electrocatalysis. Photoelectrochemistry and electrogenerated chemiluminescence.
Conductive polymers. Solid-state electrochemistry. Bioelectrochemistry.
Nanoelectrochemistry and computer simulation.

## Extension: L17–L28 (done)

The original plan stopped at L16 and folded the remaining Bagotsky chapters into
it. That material was instead written out as twelve further lessons, so the
later chapters get the same depth as the core:

### L17 — Solid-state electrochemistry · A ch.29–30
Ionic conduction in solids and solid electrolytes. Point defects and their
equilibria. Intercalation and insertion. Solid-state cells, sensors and devices.

### L18 — Conducting polymers · A ch.28
Conjugated backbones and charge carriers. Doping and the insulator-to-metal
transition. Redox and capacitive behaviour. Applications in devices and sensors.

### L19 — Studying the electrode surface · A ch.16, B ch.14
What "the surface" means at atomic scale. Real vs ideal surfaces. Adsorption and
its measurement. In-situ structural probes and the information each gives.

### L20 — Electrocatalysis · A ch.31, B ch.3.6
Why catalysts change rate but not thermodynamics. Adsorption energy and the
volcano relation. Reaction pathways, promoting and poisoning. Fuel-cell and
water-splitting catalysts.

### L21 — Bioelectrochemistry · A ch.27,35
Electrochemistry of biological molecules. Electron transfer in and between
proteins. Biosensors and mediators. Biofuel cells and biocompatible interfaces.

### L22 — Electrokinetic phenomena · A ch.23
Charge on a moving interface. Electrophoresis, electro-osmosis, streaming and
sedimentation potentials. The zeta potential. Electrokinetic characterisation.

### L23 — Liquid-liquid interfaces · A ch.24–25
The interface between two immiscible electrolyte solutions. Ion and electron
transfer across the ITIES. Distribution potentials. Polarised interfaces and
their voltammetry.

### L24 — Unusual electrochemical phenomena · A ch.26
Oscillatory and chaotic reactions. Pattern and structure formation at
electrodes. Spontaneous and current oscillations. What out-of-equilibrium
electrochemistry reveals.

### L25 — The elementary act of electron transfer · A ch.32
The single electron-transfer step. Franck-Condon and reorganisational energy.
Marcus theory and its predictions. Adiabatic vs non-adiabatic transfer.

### L26 — Computer simulation in electrochemistry · A ch.33
What simulation adds to theory. Molecular dynamics and Monte Carlo. Continuum
and finite-difference simulation of transport and kinetics. Choosing a model.

### L27 — Nanoelectrochemistry · A ch.34, B ch.18
Behaviour at the nanoscale. Nanoelectrodes and their unusual responses. Single
molecule and single particle electrochemistry. Nanostructured electrodes.

### L28 — The development of electrochemistry · A ch.36–37
From Galvani and Volta to the modern field. How the concepts in this course were
discovered. Electrochemistry in industry and its contemporary frontiers.

## Sequencing rationale

The order follows the physics rather than either book's numbering:

1. **What carries charge** (L1)
2. **What sets the potential** (L2–L3)
3. **What limits the current** (L4)
4. **What governs the rate** (L5–L6)
5. **How you measure** (L7–L12)
6. **What it is used for** (L13–L15)

L7 sits between kinetics and technique deliberately: a student who has met
Butler–Volmer but has never built a three-electrode cell will misread every
voltammogram they take.

## Decisions taken

1. **Ambition.** The full set was built: L0–L28, plus one problem set per
   Bard & Faulkner end-of-chapter set for chapters 1–17.
2. **Where the questions come from.** All prose and every question is written
   fresh and the answers are checked against the books; nothing is quoted from
   either book. The problem-set lessons are original problems written in the
   style of each chapter's exercises, attributed to the chapter but not copied
   from it.
3. **Retire the old Fundamentals primer?** Resolved: it has been removed and
   replaced by the shorter L0 above, and the lecturing proper now starts at L1.
   The fuller introductory material (conductance, activity, two kinds of
   conductor) is carried by L1–L3 instead.
